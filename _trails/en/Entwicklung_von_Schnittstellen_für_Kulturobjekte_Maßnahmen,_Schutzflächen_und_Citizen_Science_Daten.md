## Description

TRAIL 4.1 aims to facilitate the exchange and reuse of heterogeneous heritage data, which is typically stored at the state level. A key challenge is that these extensive, dynamic data collections are stored in different, often proprietary IT systems and, to date, can only be exchanged manually between institutions (e.g., heritage authorities and research institutions). This creates significant obstacles for research, documentation, and long-term data preservation. To address this, TRAIL 4.1 is developing community standards and interfaces based, among other things, on the ADeX standard of the Association of State Archaeological Offices. These define core and optional data fields, their types, and contents to enable interoperability and automated data exchange between systems. The interfaces are also intended to be semantically linked to the vocabularies of other TRAILs (particularly 4.2). The goal of the TRAIL is to create FAIR-compliant standards for find sites, interventions, protected areas, and citizen science data. Through implementation and testing in the partner institutions’ systems, a sustainable foundation will be established for the standardized, efficient, and open exchange of cultural heritage data within the framework of the NFDI.
