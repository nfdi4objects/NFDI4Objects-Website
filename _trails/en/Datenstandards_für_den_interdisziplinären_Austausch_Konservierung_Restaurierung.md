## Description

To date, conservation-restoration measures have been documented in heterogeneous, largely non-standardized formats. This lack of standardization hinders the traceability of treatment histories, (inter)disciplinary data exchange, and, consequently, comparative research. Furthermore, semantic ambiguities in the data impede technically accurate interpretation and further processing.

The TRAIL addresses these challenges by developing standardized formats that enable complex relationships in the field of conservation and restoration to be presented in a transparent and machine-readable manner. This standardized recording of practice enables the long-term identification of successful treatment strategies. Looking ahead, integration into knowledge graphs opens up the possibility of interdisciplinary networking of restoration knowledge in broader contexts.

As part of the TRAIL project, a community-based minimal metadata set is being developed and refined to identify the core elements of good documentation. In addition, the technical implementation of this standard is being carried out through the development of an interoperable exchange format (LIDO-XML) and an application ontology based on CIDOC CRM. The practical benefits of these standards will be demonstrated through their application to previously unstructured datasets and the integration of this processed data into the N4O Knowledge Graph.
