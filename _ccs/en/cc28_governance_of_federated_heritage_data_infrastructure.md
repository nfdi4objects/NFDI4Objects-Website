## Description
A significant proportion of the archaeological research data generated in Germany is collected in the context of public sector activities, in particular on behalf of the federal states and local authorities, and is stored, catalogued and preserved for the long term by the state heritage authorities. This data is of central importance for scientific use. At the same time, the federal responsibilities that are characterised by the federal states’ cultural sovereignty lead to heterogeneous legal, institutional and infrastructural frameworks, which have so far made it difficult to ensure uniform, FAIR-compliant accessibility and re-use.

Against the backdrop of the increasing relevance of the NFDI cross-cutting theme ‘Public Data’, there is a growing need for a systematic classification of these structures. The Community Cluster ‘Governance of Federated Heritage Data Infrastructures’ addresses this situation and aims to conduct a structured analysis of the conditions governing federated data infrastructures for material culture. The focus is on a comparative review of the legal frameworks in the federal states, including the respective legislative situations, institutional infrastructures and relevant guidelines regarding the publication and use of research data within the state heritage authorities.

On this basis, a strategic framework for strengthening and further developing data infrastructures could be drawn up within a technical working group (TWG) and in close consultation with the state heritage authorities, taking into account both the requirements of government bodies and the needs of the academic community. The aim would be to jointly identify viable approaches to standardisation, data exchange and sustainable infrastructure development within a federal framework, to highlight existing practices and to translate these into a coordinated further development of cooperation between the state authorities and the NFDI4Objects community.

The cluster focuses on issues relating to data exchange and cross-sectoral use. In doing so, the cluster draws on existing recommendations, in particular those of the Association of State Archaeologists, and sees itself as contributing to further coordination and networking within the context of federated data infrastructures.

## Objectives

- Joint assessment of the legal framework in the 16 federal states (including a structured overview)

- Consolidation and comparative presentation of institutional structures and existing infrastructures within the state heritage authorities

- Collection and collation of relevant regulations and guidelines on data publication and use

- (optional via spin-off into a TWG): Identifying common challenges and prospects within the federal context through dialogue with the stakeholders involved

- (optionally via a spin-off into a TWG): Development of a coordinated framework for the further development and better integration of data infrastructures

- (optionally via a spin-off into a TWG): Derivation of practical guidelines for standardisation, data exchange and sustainable infrastructure development
