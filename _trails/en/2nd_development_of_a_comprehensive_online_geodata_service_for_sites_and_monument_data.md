## Description

To improve the discoverability and usability of cultural heritage data generated by state monument offices, universities, and citizen scientists, among others, several INSPIRE- and OGC-compliant online geodata services are being developed as part of this TRAIL project. Based on the exchange interfaces developed in TRAIL 4.1, these services will cover data on cultural heritage objects, intervention objects, protected areas, and citizen science objects, and will also represent legal implications, such as those arising from the various state monument protection laws. This will enable a daily updated view of the data in the archives and systems of the data holders. To safeguard the data holders’ interests, data access is regulated through a tiered access rights system. The geodata services can be integrated into users’ own information systems via interfaces, thereby enabling cross-system data exchange.
Furthermore, within the framework of TRAIL, guidelines are being developed for the independent creation of standardized geodata services, which will enable the professional community to publish their own data in accordance with the standardized data models.
