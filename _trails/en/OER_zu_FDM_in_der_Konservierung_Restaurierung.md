## Description

The results of the survey on RDM in conservation-restoration make it clear that the community recognizes FDM as an important component of restoration research. At the same time, there is considerable uncertainty regarding their own competencies and the implementation of appropriate strategies. The lack of subject-specific documentation standards, heterogeneous data realities, and a confusing software landscape hinder the systematic and sustainable implementation of RDM practices and thus the FAIR provision of relevant restoration data.

TRAIL 4.6 addresses these challenges by developing subject-specific Open Educational Resources (OER) on RDM in conservation-restoration. The goal is to empower researchers, instructors, and students to apply RDM principles, standards, and tools confidently and consistently in their work.

TRAIL represents a collaboration between TA4 and TA6, as the subject-specific OERs are developed in accordance with the qualification framework and delivery concept established in TA6. Furthermore, to avoid duplication of effort, efforts are being made to link or supplement existing general RDM OERs from TA6 with restoration-specific examples wherever possible.
