On 26 and 27 November 2026, the conference ‘Ground Truth? Museums, Machines and the Organisation of Knowledge’ will take place at the Deutsches Museum. It will explore how museum knowledge is created, organised and processed in digital systems, and what role artificial intelligence, knowledge graphs, classifications and historical systems of knowledge play in this process.

[Sarah Wagner](/_persons/de/sarahwagner.md) (FAU Competence Centre for Research Data and Information, FAU CDI | NFDI4Objects) will present the poster ‘How is Ground Truth Created? Perspective and Information Provenance in the NFDI4Objects Object Biography’ during the poster session on 26 November, which will be introduced by short lightning talks.

### What it’s about

What can be regarded as ‘ground truth’ in digital and machine-processed collection data is one of the conference’s key questions. Information on the provenance, dating, involved actors or functions of objects is often based on sources, historical attributions and expert interpretations. It is not context-free fact.

The object biography developed in NFDI4Objects takes this into account: object information is consistently modelled as attributions and linked to sources. In this way, not only the statement itself but also its information provenance is documented. Different or contradictory statements, as well as varying degrees of certainty, can be represented explicitly and in parallel.

The technical backbone is the N4O Object Ontology, which is based on CIDOC CRM. It is being implemented and tested in the WissKI research and documentation environment using interdisciplinary use cases from collections and provenance research.

### About the conference

The programme brings together contributions on algorithmically mediated knowledge, AI-supported cataloguing, Linked Open Data and curatorial responsibility. On the second day of the conference, the focus will be on responsibility and contextualisation in the transition from cultural collections to data, as well as curatorial responsibility for algorithmic knowledge structures.
